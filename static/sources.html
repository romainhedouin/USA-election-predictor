Method & data sources

Where every number on the map comes from, and how the projections are made. Last reviewed November 3, 2026. This project is independent and not affiliated with any of the sources below.

How a projection is made

Each county (or House district) is projected from what has been counted so far, compared with how the same place voted last time. The difference is a swing, trusted more as more of the vote is in, and applied to the vote still to count. Where there is no usable earlier result, the rest of the vote is assumed to split like the vote counted so far. A state's projection is the sum of its counties'.

In any state or district drill-down, the ? next to each row shows the exact working for that projection with the real numbers, and "How this total is computed" does the same for the total. The math itself is in estimate.js; the whole project is open source.

Live results

  • NBC News election results — the only live source: every count on the map, by county (or by town, ward or district where a state reports that way) and by House district. Fetched from the same results service NBC's own pages use. Counts are unofficial until each state certifies them. Senate, governor and House show the 2026 count; President has no 2026 pages, so that tab keeps NBC's final 2024 results and says so in the line under the title.

What each race is compared against

PresidentEach county's 2024 presidential result, from tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results_08-24 (2024_US_County_Level_Presidential_Results.csv, MIT license), which compiles county returns. The MIT Election Data and Science Lab's County Presidential Election Returns 2000–2024 (CC0) is the equivalent official dataset the builder also accepts.
SenateThe state's last regular election for the same seat (2020 for most, 2022 for Florida and Ohio), from the MIT Election Data and Science Lab's U.S. Senate statewide 1976–2024 (CC0). Each county gets its own baseline: its 2024 presidential lean (above), shifted by how that Senate result differed from the state's 2024 presidential result.
HouseEach district's 2024 result, from NBC's final 2024 numbers, checked against the House Clerk's official Statistics of the Presidential and Congressional Election of November 5, 2024: 413 of 435 districts match vote for vote, and every district used is within 1.9 points of the official Democratic share (269 of 273 within 0.2). Districts redrawn for 2026, and 2024 races without both a Democrat and a Republican, have no baseline.
GovernorStatewide vote totals for each state's last governor's race, taken from each race's Wikipedia article. Totals only, with no party split, so they size the race but the projection itself uses the counted-so-far split.

Maps and boundaries

  • Counties and states: us-atlas 3.0.1 (ISC license), built from the U.S. Census Bureau's cartographic boundary files, 2017 edition.
  • House districts: the Census Bureau's 2024 cartographic 119th-Congress districts (cb_2024_us_cd119_20m), the lines of the 2024 election; for the nine states that redrew for 2026 (Alabama, California, Florida, Louisiana, North Carolina, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, Utah), the Census Bureau's 2026 TIGER/Line 120th-Congress districts. Which districts changed is worked out by comparing those with the 2025 TIGER/Line 119th-Congress lines.
  • Matching NBC's county names to Census county codes: derived from NBC's own final 2024 results, joining each county by its unique vote counts.

Which races are on the ballot

  • Electoral votes: the National Archives' 2020-census allocation (538 total).
  • 2026 Senate and governor races: 2026 United States Senate elections and 2026 United States gubernatorial elections (Wikipedia).
  • 2026 House maps: the Census Bureau's congressional districts page, 2025–2026 United States redistricting (Wikipedia), and, for Missouri keeping its 2022 lines, the Missouri Supreme Court's September 3, 2026 ruling.
